What the data say now

  • Valuation. In Sep 2026, the latest official month, the real exchange rate was 15% below its 2003–present average (the peso stronger than usual). Since then the peso has weakened from an average of 17.26 to 18.72 per dollar (Nov 2026 to date), which puts the index near 92 (estimate): 8% stronger than average, a level the peso was at or stronger than in 24% of months since January 2003. USD/MXN is 19.16 today, up 3.6% over the past year.
  • Carry. Banxico's target rate exceeds the Fed's by 2.38 percentage points. By covered interest parity this is roughly the annual forward premium on USD/MXN, i.e. the yearly cost of hedging a peso receivable by selling pesos forward (or the gain from hedging a peso payable).
  • Inflation. Mexican inflation is 3.45% versus 3.74% in the U.S. (latest common month). A positive gap erodes the peso's real value unless the nominal rate depreciates by a similar amount.
  • Trade. Over the last 12 months the non-oil goods balance was US$45.3 bn and the oil balance −US$32.4 bn. Automotive exports totaled US$189 bn, -0.5% versus the previous 12 months.
  • Risk. The 10-year Bono M–Treasury spread is 4.12 pp. Realized USD/MXN volatility is 9.8% (sample median 9.7%), so the carry earned per unit of risk is 0.24. A low ratio means an unhedged position is paid little for the currency risk it carries.
